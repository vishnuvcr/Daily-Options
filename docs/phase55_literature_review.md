# Phase 55 Literature Review

## Smile shape and jump risk

Yan (2011) shows that the slope of the implied-volatility smile can proxy jump risk and relates the option smile to subsequent returns. The paper's slope is not identical to Phase 55's curvature, so it serves as motivation for testing smile shape rather than evidence of this exact intraday rule.

Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X1000187X

## Quantifying implied-volatility smile shape

Zhang and Xiang (2008) develop an industry-standard framework for quantifying the implied-volatility smirk/smile in equity-index options. This supports measuring curvature from adjacent OTM and ATM implied volatilities.

Source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/14697680601173444

## Curvature and volatility forecasting

Reus, Carrasco and Pincheira (2020) report that traditional curvature and symmetry measures of option smiles improve volatility forecasts in currency-option markets. This is a volatility-prediction result, not evidence that NIFTY directional option trades will be profitable after friction.

Source: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v34y2020ics1544612319302831.html

## Indian smile evidence

Singh (2013) studies the information content and parabolic formulation of option volatility smiles using Indian market data, including the practical relevance of smile/skew shape for derivatives pricing.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1057/jdhf.2013.14

## Research implication

The literature supports a preregistered smile-curvature test, but not a direct claim of short-horizon NIFTY profitability. Phase 55 therefore uses a fixed adjacent-strike curvature definition, finite tercile states, permutation nulls and the same dual-friction after-cost promotion gate.
