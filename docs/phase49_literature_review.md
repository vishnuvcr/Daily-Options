# Phase 49 Literature Review

## Volatility regime and opening gaps

Steenbarger's gap study measures the opening gap relative to the previous day's high-low range specifically to normalize gap magnitude for changing volatility. This is direct motivation for conditioning the opening-gap experiment on a prior-session range regime.

Source: https://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2006/11/do-opening-gaps-tend-to-fill.html

## Contraction/expansion

Research on opening-range breakout profitability discusses the contraction-expansion principle: markets alternate between calmer and more expansive movement regimes, and breakout behavior may depend on which regime precedes the session.

Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1544612312000438

## Gap continuation and reversal

Plastun et al. (2020) examine post-gap price behavior in major US indices and report abnormal post-gap movement, motivating a direct test of both continuation and reversal rather than assuming all gaps fill.

Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1062940820300747

A 2023 open-access study of stock opening gaps finds that the size and direction of the gap are associated with different subsequent price adjustments, reinforcing that gap context can matter.

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10017064/

A 2026 study of 24 global indices finds opening-gap series display heavy tails and volatility clustering, supporting explicit volatility-regime conditioning rather than assuming a constant gap distribution.

Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214845026000918

## Research implication

The literature supports testing the volatility context of an opening gap, but does not establish after-cost profitability for NIFTY options. Phase 49 therefore uses a finite 3-state regime grid, permutation nulls, and the project's fixed Base/Stress promotion criteria.
