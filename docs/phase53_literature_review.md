# Phase 53 Literature Review

## Implied-volatility skew and return predictability

Fu et al. (2016) review option-implied volatility measures and find predictive content in measures including implied-volatility skew. Their work motivates measuring skew directly rather than absolute IV alone.

Source: https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/80351/

The 2010 Financial Analysts Journal paper by Doran and Krieger studies future asset returns linked to the call-put volatility difference and the left side of the volatility skew.

Source: https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v66.n1.9

Jha and Kalimipalli (2010) study conditional skewness in index option markets and report economic significance for skewness-based option strategies, while also noting that trading costs weaken profitability.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1002/fut.20414

## Friction and interpretation

A 2025 Journal of Financial Economics study finds that implied-volatility spreads and skew can predict returns, but much of the apparent predictability is related to omitted stock-borrow fees and the economic gains are substantially smaller once that friction is accounted for. This is directly relevant to Phase 53's dual-friction promotion gate.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2025.104153

## Research implication

These findings support a bounded test of contemporaneous near-ATM skew, but they do not establish NIFTY intraday option profitability after realistic execution costs. Phase 53 therefore uses a finite 12-cell grid, permutation nulls, strict 09:30 information timing, historical lots and the repository's Base/Stress friction model.
